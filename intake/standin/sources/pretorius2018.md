Pretorius, J. (2018). *Ventilation commissioning and its aftermath in school
retrofits*. Cape Meridian University Press. ISBN 978-0-000000-18-4.

Commissioning is repeatedly described in the retrofit literature as a discrete event.
Pretorius argues that it is better understood as the first point in a maintenance
sequence, and that most of the performance loss observed in the studied buildings
occurred after handover rather than at it.

In the 18 schools followed for three years, 12 had at least one control setting changed
by site staff without a record of the change. In seven of those cases the change
defeated a demand-controlled ventilation strategy that the retrofit had installed.

Where changes were recorded, they were recorded in a maintenance log that was not
shared with the design team. Pretorius describes this as a structural rather than an
individual failure: no party to the contract had a duty to reconcile as-built settings
against design intent after the defects liability period.

Occupant complaints were the earliest available signal of a defeated strategy, preceding
any energy signature by an average of five months. The author treats complaint logs as
an underused diagnostic and recommends they be read alongside the building management
system trend data rather than as a service matter.

Rebalancing after a fabric retrofit is recommended as standard, because envelope
improvements change the pressure regime the ventilation system was balanced against.
Pretorius reports that only 4 of the 18 schools had been rebalanced.

The study's follow-up ended at three years. The author is explicit that no claim is made
about longer-run persistence, and that the three-year window was set by funding rather
than by any expectation about when settings stabilise.
