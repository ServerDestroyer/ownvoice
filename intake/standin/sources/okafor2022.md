Okafor, C. (2022). *What post-occupancy comfort surveys can and cannot establish: a
meta-analysis of 74 studies*. Synthetic Reviews in Built Environment, 9, 1-38.
Companion methods note published the same year as Okafor (2022a).

Seventy-four studies met the inclusion criteria, of which 61 reported enough detail for
effect size extraction. The pooled association between envelope retrofit and improved
comfort vote was positive but small, and the prediction interval crossed zero.

Heterogeneity was high and largely unexplained by climate zone, building age, or
retrofit depth. The single moderator that reduced heterogeneity was whether the survey
was administered in the same season as the pre-retrofit baseline; studies that mixed
seasons reported systematically larger effects.

Okafor finds evidence of small-study effects consistent with selective reporting.
Trim-and-fill adjustment reduces the pooled estimate by roughly a third, though the
author cautions that the method assumes a symmetry that may not hold here.

Only nine of the 74 studies reported a pre-registered analysis plan. Okafor argues that
in the absence of pre-registration the distinction between a confirmatory and an
exploratory comfort finding cannot be recovered from the published record.

The review declines to convert its findings into a design recommendation. Okafor's
position is that the literature can currently support claims about measurement practice
and cannot yet support claims about which retrofit strategies improve comfort.

A companion methods note (Okafor, 2022a) documents the extraction protocol, the two
independent coders, and the disagreement resolution procedure. Inter-coder agreement was
0.81 on study design and 0.62 on retrofit depth, the latter described as marginal.
