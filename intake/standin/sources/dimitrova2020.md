# Dimitrova et al. — instrumentation and sampling for classroom comfort studies

> SYNTHETIC stand-in source. This work does not exist. Written for OwnVoice
> development (build step T4). Locator sidecar: `dimitrova2020.locators.json`.

Dimitrova, S., Lindqvist, P. & Rahman, A. (2020). *Instrumentation and sampling for
classroom comfort studies: a practitioner handbook*. Meridian Technical Publishing,
3rd edition. ISBN 978-0-000000-20-0.

Sensors should be positioned at seated head height, 1.1 m above finished floor level for
primary-age occupants, and no closer than 1 m to an external wall or 0.5 m to a
mechanical supply diffuser. Positions must be photographed and recorded, because
post-hoc reconstruction of sensor placement is the most common cause of unusable
archives.

Globe temperature requires a settling period. The handbook specifies a minimum of
twenty minutes after any repositioning before a reading is recorded, and warns that
readings taken during the settling period will bias the mean radiant estimate upward in
sunlit rooms.

Sampling should cover both occupied and unoccupied hours. The recommended minimum is
two consecutive teaching weeks per season, logged at five-minute intervals, with at
least one week falling outside a school holiday period.

Calibration drift is treated as a scheduled cost rather than a risk. The handbook
requires a pre-deployment and post-deployment calibration check against a reference
instrument, and states that a study reporting only a pre-deployment check has no basis
for claiming measurement accuracy at the end of the campaign.

Occupancy counts must be logged independently of the comfort survey. Where occupancy is
inferred from CO2 concentration alone, the handbook requires that the inference method
and its validation be reported, since the relationship is sensitive to ventilation rate.

Where a survey and a measurement campaign run together, votes should be timestamped to
the minute and matched to the nearest logged interval. Matching votes to a daily mean is
described as a common and avoidable error that destroys most of the analytical value of
the survey.

The handbook's checklists are reproduced in Appendix C and are intended to be attached
to a study's data management plan rather than summarised in prose.
