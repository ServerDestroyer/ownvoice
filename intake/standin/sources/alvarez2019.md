Alvarez, R. (2019). *Occupant comfort reports in retrofitted primary schools: a
two-season field survey*. Northfield Academic Press, 2nd edition. DOI 10.0000/synth.9019.

The study surveyed 41 primary schools in a temperate maritime climate, all of which had
received envelope retrofits within the preceding six years. Comfort votes were collected
on the seven-point thermal sensation scale during two measurement seasons, giving 3,180
paired votes from 1,062 occupants. Response rates were higher in the heating season.

Occupant votes diverged from the modelled predictions in 34% of classroom-hours. The
divergence was not symmetric: occupants reported discomfort where the model predicted
neutrality about twice as often as the reverse. Alvarez attributes the asymmetry to
localised radiant effects that the whole-room model does not resolve.

Divergence was concentrated in rooms with large south-facing glazing and in rooms whose
mechanical ventilation had been commissioned but not rebalanced after the retrofit. In
the remaining rooms, model and vote agreed within one scale point for 88% of hours.

The survey instrument was administered on paper in the first season and on tablets in
the second. Alvarez reports no significant difference in mean vote between the two
administration modes, but notes that the tablet condition produced fewer partially
completed forms and that the comparison was not pre-registered.

Teachers, rather than pupils, were the respondents in 29 of the 41 schools. Where both
groups responded, adult votes were consistently one third of a scale point warmer than
pupil votes for the same room-hour. The study treats this as a limitation rather than a
finding, because the pupil sample was small and clustered in four schools.

Alvarez recommends that comfort surveys be repeated after any change to ventilation
control settings, and argues that a single post-occupancy survey conducted in one season
is insufficient evidence for a claim about a retrofit's comfort performance.

The dataset is available on request but was not deposited in a public repository. The
author notes that consent forms did not cover redistribution, and that this constrains
any reanalysis by third parties.
